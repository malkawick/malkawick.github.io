Malka Wickramarathna

I am an undergraduate researcher majoring in Electronic and Telecommunication Engineering at the University of Moratuwa. Alongside my formal studies, I independently explore Theoretical Physics and Biology, as I believe that technological advancements should be in harmony with natural challenges and phenomena.

My mission is to bridge the gap between fundamental scientific principles and applied engineering innovation. I am particularly interested in learning from nature and applying its principles to optimize man-made systems. I am equally (maybe more) fascinated by the inverse: utilizing existing technologies to further discover the secrets of nature. My research goal is to leverage our current technologies to uncover truths about fundamental governing principles, and to understand how we can use that knowledge for the betterment of humanity.

When I am not working toward this mission, you can find me playing my upright piano.

Technical Projects

  • Electrocoagulation Water Purifier
    Engineering Design Project | Jan 2026 – Jul 2026
    Led a four-member team, conducting literature research and designing the purification hardware for an automated, sensor-based household water purification system. We utilized an Aluminum-based electrocoagulation method aimed specifically at addressing the Chronic Kidney Disease of unknown etiology (CKDu) crisis in Sri Lanka's dry zones. We built a proof-of-concept TRL-4 prototype and validated the functionality using synthetic groundwater in a laboratory setting.
  • Project Phoenix (CubeSatellite)
    Electronic Club of UoM & SEDS Mora | Oct 2025 – Jan 2026
    Developed the Ground Station User Interface (UI) for a high-altitude weather balloon using Python and HTML for real-time telemetry visualization. I also collaborated on the validation and testing of the LoRA-based communication sub-system.
  • Smart Navigation System for Extreme Environments
    Design Project | Sept 2025 – Dec 2025
    Designed an ESP32-based smart hiking pole integrating GPS and IMU sensors for location tracking where conventional navigation fails. We developed a dead-reckoning algorithm to generate return routes and engineered a functional breadboard prototype to validate the signal acquisition.
